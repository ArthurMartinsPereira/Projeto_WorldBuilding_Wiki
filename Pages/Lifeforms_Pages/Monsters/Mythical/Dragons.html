Dragons

Higher Lifeforms Born Through Elemental Metamorphosis

Dragons

Classification Higher Magical Lifeform
Origin Magical Lifeforms
Reproduction None
Diet Magical Energy
Lifespan Potentially Indefinite
Intelligence Highly Intelligent
Elemental Affinity One or More Elements
Known Examples Vaelkros, Fafnir, Spiral Terror, Sun Eater, The Purple Tide, White Wyrm

Overview

Dragons are not a single species, but rather a classification given to certain lifeforms that have undergone a mysterious metamorphosis, transforming into extraordinarily powerful beings. Any biological lifeform capable of manipulating elemental energy may potentially undergo this process, with the only known exceptions being humanoid and sapient races. This includes not only animals, but theoretically also plants and fungi.

Every dragon is unique. Even individuals originating from the same species can develop vastly different appearances, abilities, temperaments, and behaviors. Some retain many characteristics of their original forms, while others undergo such extensive changes that their origins become difficult to recognize.

Despite their differences, all dragons share a few defining characteristics. They possess a Draconic Nucleus, an organ or structure that allows them to store and manipulate magical energy, possess exceptional control over their powers, and are capable of sustaining themselves through the consumption of elemental energy.

Dragons are among the most powerful naturally occurring lifeforms in Drakma. Some have existed for thousands of years, while others have left permanent marks on the lands they inhabit. Their emergence is relatively rare, but the consequences can be severe enough to affect entire regions and civilizations.

Metamorphosis

The process through which a lifeform becomes a dragon remains one of the great mysteries of the natural world. Neither its exact cause nor its underlying mechanisms are fully understood. What is known comes primarily from observations of individuals undergoing the transformation and the environmental changes that accompany it.

Before the metamorphosis begins, an affected lifeform may exhibit unusual behavioral changes. It will gradually stop eating and sleeping, eventually abandoning its usual habits in favor of searching for a secluded location with a strong concentration of elemental energy. Once a suitable location is found, the lifeform will settle down and enter a deep, prolonged sleep.

A chrysalis will slowly begin to form around its body, eventually enveloping it completely. The transformation itself is exceptionally slow, sometimes taking decades or even centuries to reach completion. During this period, the developing dragon gradually changes within its chrysalis, drawing upon the surrounding elemental energy.

As the transformation progresses, the developing dragon begins to influence the local ley lines. Its presence gradually alters their flow, directing increasingly large amounts of elemental energy toward its location. This phenomenon can have severe consequences for the surrounding environment, sometimes changing entire ecosystems or even affecting large geographical regions.

Once the metamorphosis is complete, the dragon emerges from its chrysalis. It may remain in the location where it developed, eventually turning it into its lair, or leave in search of a more suitable territory, particularly if the surrounding ley lines have been heavily depleted.

The environmental conditions surrounding a chrysalis may influence the developing dragon, particularly its elemental affinities. However, the dragon itself generally has a much greater influence on the local ley lines than the environment has on its development.

Appearance and Anatomy

There is no standardized appearance for dragons. Their physical characteristics vary considerably depending on their original species and the changes brought about by their metamorphosis. While many develop imposing bodies and features commonly associated with dragons, others retain a surprisingly close resemblance to their original forms.

The Spiral Terror, for example, retains much of the appearance of its original sandworm form, while the White Wyrm remains relatively similar to the Cave Wyrm from which it originated. Other dragons undergo much more dramatic transformations, developing entirely new anatomical structures and physical characteristics.

The Draconic Nucleus

The one anatomical feature shared by all dragons is the Draconic Nucleus. This specialized organ or structure is responsible for storing and manipulating magical energy, serving as the foundation of a dragon's extraordinary abilities.

Despite being a universal characteristic, the Nucleus varies greatly in appearance and location. In some dragons it takes the form of an internal organ, while in others it may be integrated into an external structure, such as a horn or another appendage. Its placement and physical characteristics appear to be unique to each individual.

The Nucleus is both a dragon's greatest source of power and its most significant vulnerability. Although damaging or reaching it is an exceptionally difficult task, its destruction can result in the dragon's defeat.

The Nuclei of fallen dragons are highly valuable. Throughout history, some legendary weapons and artifacts have been forged using these structures, taking advantage of their extraordinary magical properties.

Abilities

Dragons are among the most refined natural manipulators of magic in the world. Their control over elemental energy is exceptionally precise, allowing them to wield their abilities with a degree of mastery rarely observed outside of humanity's most advanced magical practitioners.

Elemental Affinities

Most dragons possess at least one elemental affinity, although some individuals can develop several. Unlike other known lifeforms, dragons are naturally capable of sustaining multiple elemental affinities without suffering the usual drawbacks associated with combining different elements.

A dragon's original elemental affinity generally remains after its metamorphosis. However, developing additional affinities is possible, while replacing an existing one appears to be considerably more difficult. The elemental energy available during the metamorphosis may also influence the final result.

Dragons are the only known natural lifeforms capable of maintaining multiple elemental affinities without apparent adverse effects. The Arcane element is the sole known exception: no dragon has ever been observed wielding it. Considering the already extraordinary power of these creatures, this may be fortunate.

Physical and Magical Development

Dragons can grow stronger as they age, although this is not necessarily a gradual or guaranteed process. Their development largely depends on the amount of elemental energy they consume throughout their lives.

Older dragons that have access to abundant sources of elemental energy can become extraordinarily powerful, potentially surpassing younger individuals by a considerable margin. Their abilities and physical characteristics may also continue to develop over time.

Behavior and Intelligence

Dragons are highly intelligent lifeforms, capable of learning and adapting their behavior through experience. Their intelligence is comparable to that of particularly intelligent mammals or birds, allowing them to recognize threats, develop hunting strategies, and remember individuals or locations.

However, dragons are not sapient. They do not possess the same capacity for abstract reasoning and communication associated with humanoid and other sapient races. They cannot speak or communicate through language, and their behavior remains largely influenced by instinct, temperament, and individual experience.

Their temperaments vary considerably. Some are relatively passive and prefer to remain undisturbed within their territories, while others can be extremely aggressive or territorial. Their reactions to intruders and perceived threats differ from one individual to another.

Dragons generally prefer to remain within their lairs and rarely leave unless they are threatened, driven away, or forced to search for a new territory due to a lack of available elemental energy.

Although dragons primarily feed on magical energy, some continue to hunt and consume other living organisms. This behavior is believed to be a remnant of the instincts inherited from their original forms.

Ecology

The presence of a dragon can have a profound influence on its surroundings. During the metamorphosis, the developing dragon can drastically alter the local ley lines, often resulting in significant environmental changes. These effects may persist long after the chrysalis has disappeared.

Even after emerging, dragons continue to influence their surroundings through their consumption and manipulation of elemental energy. However, the long-term consequences of their presence are not always entirely negative.

If a dragon remains in a region for several centuries, the local environment may gradually adapt to its presence. Plants, animals, and even the surrounding landscape can develop characteristics suited to the altered magical conditions. In some cases, a dragon's continuous consumption of elemental energy can even help stabilize an otherwise hostile environment.

Nevertheless, living near a dragon is always dangerous. Even relatively passive individuals can become unpredictable when disturbed or provoked, and the consequences of angering one can be catastrophic.

Dragons cannot reproduce, meaning every new individual must originate through another lifeform undergoing the metamorphosis. Their emergence is therefore an exceptionally rare occurrence, generally taking place only once every few years or decades.

As long as a dragon continues to consume sufficient magical energy, it can theoretically survive indefinitely. The oldest known individuals are already thousands of years old, although the actual limits of their lifespan remain unknown.

Dragon Emergence

The emergence of a new dragon is a significant event, often attracting the attention of local governments, scholars, and organizations such as the Hunters Guild. When a chrysalis is discovered, the usual response is to eliminate it before the metamorphosis can be completed.

Preventing the emergence of a dragon is generally considered preferable to confronting a fully developed individual. However, destroying a chrysalis is not without risks. Even before completing its transformation, the developing creature can already possess considerable power and should never be underestimated.

The metamorphosis can also be interrupted by natural factors, potentially forcing a premature emergence. Such cases can result in imperfectly developed individuals, often referred to as pseudo-dragons. Their condition and capabilities vary, but even these incomplete dragons can be extremely dangerous.

Not every pseudo-dragon behaves in the same way. Some may attempt to flee from their surroundings, while others can stand their ground and defend themselves aggressively. Their incomplete development makes them particularly unpredictable.

Despite the efforts of the Hunters Guild and other organizations to monitor potential cases, new dragons continue to emerge. Their rarity and the difficulty of detecting a developing chrysalis mean that preventing every emergence is impossible.

Relationship with Civilizations

Dragons have been responsible for some of the greatest disasters recorded throughout Drakma's history. Their immense power and ability to influence the environment have resulted in the destruction of settlements, cities, and even entire civilizations.

However, dragons are not inherently hostile toward humanoid populations. Most prefer to remain within their territories and will not actively seek out settlements unless their behavior or circumstances bring them into conflict with people.

Attacks on settlements are often associated with newly emerged or displaced dragons. Individuals that have recently completed their metamorphosis may be unfamiliar with their surroundings, while those forced to abandon their former lairs may search for new territories that are already inhabited.

Because of the immense danger involved, most governments and organizations prefer to avoid confronting established dragons whenever possible. If an individual remains within its territory and does not pose an immediate threat to nearby populations, it is generally safer to monitor its movements and maintain a sufficient distance.

The Hunters Guild is among the organizations responsible for monitoring dragon activity, investigating reports of developing chrysalises, and coordinating responses to potential threats. Its involvement does not always result in an attempt to kill the dragon, as the consequences of such an operation may be considerably worse than allowing the creature to remain undisturbed.

The Spiral Terror is a notable example of this situation. It inhabits the center of the Spiral Valley, rarely leaving its territory. Although it poses a threat to sand ships and caravans that approach too closely, its continuous consumption of magical energy weakens the perpetual storm at the heart of the region. This has made the surrounding climate more manageable and allowed settlements to exist in an otherwise hostile environment.

Notable Dragons

Throughout history, several dragons have become particularly well known due to their immense power, unusual characteristics, or the consequences of their presence. Among the most notable recorded individuals are:

  • Vaelkros: A dragon associated with the freezing of the region surrounding the former Northwind capital, Windrise.
  • Fafnir: One of the recognized dragons recorded in Drakma's history.
  • Spiral Terror: A dragon originating from a sandworm, inhabiting the center of the Spiral Valley.
  • Sun Eater: A notable dragon known by this title.
  • The Purple Tide: A dragon known by this name.
  • The White Wyrm / White Serpent: A dragon originating from a Cave Wyrm, retaining much of its original form.

Trivia

  • Dragons are not a biological species, but a classification encompassing lifeforms that have undergone a specific magical metamorphosis.
  • Even plants and fungi may theoretically undergo the transformation, provided they possess the ability to manipulate elemental energy.
  • The Draconic Nucleus is the only known anatomical feature shared by all dragons, although its form and location vary between individuals.
  • No dragon has ever been observed possessing an affinity for the Arcane element.
  • Unlike most other powerful magical lifeforms, dragons can sustain multiple elemental affinities without apparent drawbacks.
  • Primordial Dragons are distinct from modern dragons and are considered a separate subject of study.
