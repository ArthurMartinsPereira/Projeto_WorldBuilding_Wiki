Chimeras

An extremely diverse category of artificial monsters, capable of adapting to different environments through the expression and acquisition of biological traits.

Overview

Chimeras are a broad category of artificial monsters originally developed through magical biological experimentation. Rather than representing a single species, the term refers to organisms created through the combination of biological traits from multiple creatures. Their appearances, abilities and behaviors can vary considerably depending on their composition.

The first Chimeras were accidental creations resulting from early attempts to develop artificial lifeforms. These initial specimens were extremely unstable and rarely survived for long. However, following the discovery of Primeval Marrow and the development of more advanced Bioalchemy, researchers eventually managed to create stable and self-sustaining organisms.

Chimeras are particularly known for their adaptability and regenerative capabilities, making them difficult to eliminate. Although most cannot reproduce conventionally, they possess a unique biological process known as Chimeric Mitosis, which allows them to produce new individuals.

Despite their artificial origins, many Chimeras have managed to establish themselves in the wild. By inheriting the instincts and adaptations of the creatures used in their creation, they can occupy a variety of ecological niches. Although some populations can become invasive, others have gradually integrated into their environments.

Appearance

The appearance of Chimeras varies considerably depending on the creatures from which they inherit their traits. Generally, these traits originate from animals of a similar or smaller size than the Chimera itself. There are exceptions, but incorporating characteristics from significantly larger creatures is uncommon due to the biological instability associated with incompatible traits.

Their bodies can be covered in scales, fur, feathers, smooth or rubbery skin, bone plating and other structures. They may possess additional limbs, unusual appendages or multiple heads. Some particularly rare individuals even exhibit characteristics inherited from magical creatures.

As Chimeras age, their existing traits can develop further, often giving them a more primitive or savage appearance. This phenomenon is particularly noticeable in medium and large specimens, which may develop exaggerated claws, horns, scales and other physical characteristics.

In rare circumstances, particularly old individuals may acquire new biological traits by consuming other creatures. These traits can eventually be incorporated into their biological composition and passed down to subsequent generations through Chimeric Mitosis.

Biology

Primeval Marrow

Primeval Marrow is a unique biological material essential to the existence of Chimeras. Its original origins remain unknown, although it was eventually discovered during the early development of Bioalchemy. One of its most remarkable properties is its ability to absorb, store and preserve biological information, as well as produce more of itself.

Through the use of Primeval Marrow, researchers eventually developed a method of creating stable Chimeras capable of maintaining and expressing biological traits from multiple creatures. It also allows them to reproduce independently and gradually modify their biological composition.

Within a Chimera, Primeval Marrow is circulated throughout the body, carrying stored biological information to its different tissues and organs. This process is regulated by a specialized organ responsible for storing and distributing the material.

Primeval Marrow is also highly valuable as an alchemical material and remains one of the main reasons why Chimeras are targeted by poachers and illegal researchers.

Chimeric Embryos

Chimeric Embryos are the earliest stage of a Chimera's development. Unlike conventional embryos, they consist of an amorphous mass of cells and Primeval Marrow capable of rapidly developing into a complete organism.

Originally, these embryos were cultivated inside massive containment vessels and specialized incubators. However, following the development of Chimeric Mitosis, it became possible for an embryo to develop inside an existing individual before eventually separating from it.

This process is relatively quick compared to conventional gestation, usually taking anywhere from several days to a few weeks. However, it requires considerable amounts of energy from the individual carrying the embryo, leaving them weakened and vulnerable for some time afterward.

Newly separated Chimeras are generally much smaller and weaker than their parent, requiring time to grow and develop. The individual that produced the embryo also needs a considerable recovery period before it can undergo Chimeric Mitosis again.

Trait Expression and Adaptation

One of the defining characteristics of Chimeras is their ability to express and modify biological traits inherited from different creatures. However, they cannot express an unlimited number of characteristics simultaneously. Instead, their bodies prioritize the traits most beneficial to their current environment, while others remain dormant.

The number of traits a Chimera can express depends largely on its size category. Larger individuals generally possess greater biological capacity, allowing them to maintain more complex combinations of characteristics.

  • Small: Can express up to 4 traits.
  • Medium: Can express up to 6 traits.
  • Large: Can express 9 or more traits.

Not all traits occupy the same amount of biological capacity. Some may involve extensive physical structures, while others are relatively minor or internal, such as specialized organs, enhanced senses or behavioral instincts. Consequently, the number of traits an individual can express may vary.

Traits are not necessarily permanent in their expressed state. When a Chimera inhabits an environment where certain characteristics are no longer useful, they may become dormant, allowing other traits to take their place. Should environmental conditions change, previously dormant traits can become active again.

This adaptation is not an instantaneous or intelligent process. Depending on the individual and the changes in its environment, adaptation can take months or even years. In some cases, the parent might retain its existing characteristics, while its offspring are born with traits better suited to their surroundings.

Chimeras generally inherit traits from creatures of a similar or smaller size. Smaller individuals, for example, usually express characteristics originating from small creatures, although traits from medium-sized animals are also possible. Inheriting characteristics from significantly larger organisms is less common.

Trait Inheritance and Acquisition

Most Chimeras inherit their biological traits from their parent, although these characteristics can develop and change over successive generations. In rare circumstances, two individuals belonging to different Chimera lineages can voluntarily exchange some of their biological information through physical contact.

Older individuals may also acquire new traits by regularly consuming other creatures. The exact mechanism behind this phenomenon remains poorly understood, and it is currently unknown whether Chimeras can consciously select which characteristics they acquire.

Newly acquired traits can be passed down to subsequent generations through Chimeric Mitosis. However, there are limits to how much biological information a lineage can preserve. When this capacity is exceeded, older or less frequently expressed traits may become permanently lost, making room for new characteristics.

Not every inherited trait provides a practical advantage. Since adaptation is not an intelligent process, some individuals retain characteristics that serve little purpose in their current form or environment.

Behavior

Chimeras inherit most of their behavioral characteristics from the creatures used in their creation. Because of this, they generally behave more like ordinary animals than many other artificial monsters, such as Curse Beasts. However, inheriting instincts from multiple species can sometimes result in conflicting behaviors, leading to unusual or unpredictable actions.

Their social behavior varies considerably between individuals and lineages. Some are solitary and highly territorial, while others form packs with similar Chimeras. In certain cases, these groups are led by an elder individual, often the one responsible for producing the others through Chimeric Mitosis.

Chimeras belonging to different lineages are generally hostile toward one another. Despite this, some individuals may temporarily cooperate or establish contact, occasionally resulting in the exchange of biological information.

Their lifespans vary considerably depending on their biological composition. Some species only survive for a few years, while others can live for decades or even centuries. Once they become sufficiently old, they eventually lose the ability to undergo Chimeric Mitosis, although they can continue living normally.

Chimeric Mitosis

Chimeric Mitosis is the primary reproductive method of Chimeras. Through this process, an individual develops a Chimeric Embryo inside its own body, which eventually separates and emerges as a new organism.

The process requires considerable amounts of energy and leaves the individual weakened for some time. The newly produced Chimera is also considerably smaller and weaker than its parent, requiring time to grow and develop.

Although Chimeras are generally incapable of conventional sexual reproduction, different lineages can occasionally exchange biological information through physical contact. This process involves the transfer of small quantities of Primeval Marrow between individuals, allowing some of their stored traits to be exchanged.

Such exchanges are relatively rare, as Chimeras belonging to different lineages are usually hostile toward one another. Additionally, both individuals must belong to the same size category for the exchange to occur.

Fusion

An extremely rare and poorly understood phenomenon, Fusion occurs when two Chimeras attempt to exchange biological information but are incompatible with one another. Instead of successfully exchanging their Primeval Marrow, one individual absorbs the other.

The resulting organism is an unstable amalgamation of both individuals, often displaying chaotic combinations of biological structures. Multiple heads, limbs and other appendages may develop in seemingly random arrangements, making these creatures considerably different from ordinary Chimeras.

Fused individuals may exhibit up to four different elemental affinities, exceeding the usual limitations of regular Chimeras. However, their bodies are extremely unstable, and they are generally incapable of reproducing.

The fusion process places an enormous strain on the resulting organism. Most fused Chimeras survive only for a short period before their bodies begin to deteriorate, eventually melting into a mass of biological material and Primeval Marrow.

Due to their rarity and unpredictable nature, very little is known about the exact mechanisms behind Fusion.

Habitat

Chimeras are highly adaptable and can survive in a wide variety of environments, provided they possess the necessary biological adaptations. Some individuals may even carry dormant traits that become active in response to environmental pressures.

However, their adaptation is not instantaneous. While some individuals can gradually modify their expressed traits, others may remain poorly suited to their surroundings. In certain cases, only their offspring will possess the adaptations necessary to thrive in a new environment.

Due to inheriting the instincts and characteristics of different animals, many Chimeras are capable of occupying ecological niches similar to those of naturally occurring creatures. Although their presence does not always represent a serious threat to the surrounding environment, some populations can become invasive and disrupt local ecosystems.

Abilities

  • Adaptability
  • Enhanced Regeneration
  • Elemental Abilities
  • Chimeric Mitosis
  • Trait Inheritance and Acquisition
  • Biological Specialization

Chimeras generally possess up to two different elemental affinities, inherited from magical creatures used in their creation. These can include creatures such as Unicorns, Dire Wolves, Drakes and Wyverns.

Fused individuals are an exception, potentially possessing up to four elemental affinities due to the combination of their biological compositions.

Ecology

In modern times, Chimeras have become a considerably smaller threat than they once were. The development of more effective methods of elimination and containment has allowed their populations to decline to manageable levels.

Many of the lineages that have endured also appear to have gradually integrated into their environments, occupying ecological niches and establishing a more sustainable relationship with nature. However, some populations can still become invasive, particularly when their reproductive capabilities and adaptability allow them to spread into new territories.

The Hunters Guild regularly monitors known populations, keeping track of potential outbreaks and organizing extermination campaigns whenever necessary to prevent their numbers from becoming unmanageable.

Relationship with Humans

Chimeras were originally created as an unintended byproduct of early attempts to develop artificial lifeforms through Bioalchemy. Although the first specimens were unstable, researchers eventually refined their methods following the discovery of Primeval Marrow, leading to the creation of the Chimeras known today.

By the time the War of the Three Crowns began, Chimeras had already become considerably more sophisticated. Their adaptability and reproductive capabilities, however, made them difficult to control. Despite these risks, the Rebel side eventually resorted to using them as weapons, largely out of desperation.

Following the war, Chimeras and many other creations of Bioalchemy were classified as forbidden magic. Most existing research concerning Primeval Marrow, Chimeric Embryos and their biological properties was destroyed. The remaining records were sealed within the Forbidden Archives of the Magic Academy, where access is restricted to the Archmages.

Unfortunately, the prohibition did little to eliminate the interest surrounding these creatures. Since Chimeras can produce their own embryos and contain valuable Primeval Marrow, they have become highly sought after by the Black Market. Poachers and illegal hunters regularly attempt to capture or kill them, hoping to extract their Marrow and recover any Chimeric Embryos they can find.

These materials can be used to create artificial Chimeras, which can be considerably more dangerous than naturally born individuals. Unlike their wild counterparts, artificially cultivated specimens can be deliberately designed around specific traits and abilities, particularly with the assistance of modern magical and technological developments.

Some researchers use specialized magitech to influence or control their creations, although the results vary considerably. Even artificially conditioned Chimeras remain unpredictable, and particularly aggressive individuals may eventually turn against their creators.

Trivia

  • Some Chimeras inherit reproductive characteristics from their component species, even when those characteristics serve no practical purpose. For example, Griffins may retain the ability to lay eggs from their bid side, although these eggs cannot produce offspring.
  • Despite their unusual reproductive abilities, Chimeras still require food and other resources to sustain themselves, particularly when undergoing Chimeric Mitosis.
