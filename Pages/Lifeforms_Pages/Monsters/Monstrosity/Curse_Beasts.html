Overview

Curse Beasts are a category of artificial monstrosities originating from an ancient magical experiment. They were first created during the War of the Three Crowns, when rebel forces sought a way to weaponize curses without exposing their own spellcasters to the dangers of using them.

These creatures were designed to be unleashed against enemy formations, where they would charge into battle and spread deadly or debilitating curses through their attacks. However, they proved extremely difficult to control and would frequently attack anyone nearby, including their supposed allies.

Their creators expected the backlash caused by spreading such powerful curses to eventually kill the creatures. Instead, many survived, gradually adapting to their condition. Over time, the original curse developed the ability to infect other living beings, eventually giving rise to the various were-creatures and, ultimately, the Curse Beasts known today.

Unlike most artificial creatures, Curse Beasts are not simply independent organisms. Their existence revolves around the curse within them, which drives their behavior, influences their development, and allows them to reproduce by infecting other creatures.

Appearance

The appearance of Curse Beasts varies considerably depending on their particular manifestation. Some retain recognizable features from the animals they originated from, while others develop increasingly unnatural and monstrous characteristics.

The original lycanthropic manifestation produces creatures with canine or wolf-like features, commonly known as Werewolves. Other manifestations can be far more unusual, including Werehogs, Werebears, Vulturemen, and Wendigos.

Even individuals belonging to the same manifestation can differ considerably. Some appear relatively close to their original animal forms, while others become heavily mutated, developing additional limbs, distorted proportions, or other unnatural features.

Infection and Transformation

Curse Beasts reproduce by infecting other living creatures. Although animals can also become infected, humans are particularly vulnerable to the curse and tend to remain infected for much longer than other hosts.

The transformation is gradual and can take a considerable amount of time. During its early stages, the infected individual retains much of their original consciousness, although their mood and behavior begin to change. Increasing aggression, irritability, and other unusual tendencies are common symptoms.

As the curse progresses, the infected individual begins experiencing occasional transformations into a monstrous form. These episodes are usually temporary at first, with the victim eventually returning to their original appearance. However, they rarely remember anything that happened while transformed.

Each transformation leaves its mark. Over time, permanent physical changes begin appearing across the victim's body, while the temporary transformations become increasingly severe and frequent. Eventually, the individual undergoes a final transformation and never returns to their previous form.

At this point, the transformation is complete. Whatever consciousness or identity the victim once possessed is gone, leaving behind a fully developed Curse Beast. The creature retains no meaningful memories or personality from its former life and is instead driven by the instincts and compulsions imposed by the curse.

The different manifestations of the curse are commonly grouped under the term Were-Creatures. Originally, the infection was primarily lycanthropic, but as it spread to other animals, it developed new forms. Although these manifestations share a common origin, they can differ greatly in appearance, abilities, and behavior.

Behavior

Most Curse Beasts are extremely aggressive and hostile towards anything that does not belong to their own kind. Humans, animals, and other monsters are all potential targets, although their behavior can vary considerably between manifestations.

Their aggression towards humans is not entirely instinctive. The original curse was specifically designed to target humanity, and this directive remains deeply embedded within it. Infected individuals gradually develop an overwhelming compulsion to attack humans, often entering a frenzy when encountering them.

Most manifestations are not particularly intelligent and rely primarily on aggression and predatory instincts. However, older individuals can become surprisingly cunning hunters, learning to exploit their surroundings and the weaknesses of their prey. Certain manifestations also possess abilities that allow them to employ much more elaborate hunting strategies.

Their primary means of spreading the curse is through physical attacks. Most do not appear to deliberately infect their victims, instead causing infection as an unintended consequence of their aggression. Some manifestations, however, display more unusual behaviors and seem better adapted to exploiting vulnerable targets.

Curse Beasts generally survive for up to ten years after their transformation. The curse gradually takes its toll on their bodies and souls, eventually leaving them as little more than deteriorating husks. A few individuals manage to adapt to their condition and survive considerably longer.

Notable Manifestations

Werewolves

The original manifestation of the curse, Werewolves are among the most recognizable types of Curse Beasts. They possess predominantly canine features and are known for their physical strength, aggression, and predatory instincts.

Wendigos

Wendigos are among the more cunning manifestations of the curse. Rather than relying entirely on physical strength, they can manipulate the emotional state of nearby individuals through a combination of magic and infrasound.

Their presence can induce feelings of paranoia, fear, and aggression, sometimes causing groups of people to turn against one another. While their victims become increasingly distressed and disorganized, the Wendigo waits for an opportunity to attack, often targeting those who become isolated from the rest of the group.

Vulturemen

Vulturemen are particularly opportunistic predators. They generally avoid healthy prey, instead targeting individuals who are sick, injured, exhausted, or otherwise close to death. Lost travelers and struggling caravans are especially vulnerable to their attacks.

They are particularly common in the deserts of Dragonfell, where they frequently prey on caravans that have lost their way or become stranded. Vulturemen generally attempt to consume their victims, but those who survive an attack have a high chance of becoming infected with the curse.

Other manifestations include Werehogs, Werebears, and several lesser-known variants. Many remain poorly documented, especially those found in remote or particularly dangerous regions.

Habitat

Curse Beasts can inhabit almost any environment, with different manifestations possessing adaptations suited to their surroundings. They have been observed in forests, jungles, deserts, swamps, mountains, and even in the skies.

The seas remain one of the few environments where their presence is relatively uncommon, although this may simply be due to the difficulty of identifying or studying aquatic manifestations.

Across Drakma, Curse Beasts are most commonly encountered in the central and western regions of the continent. However, their range has expanded considerably over the last century, with sightings becoming increasingly common in both Falkore and Northwind.

Their ability to infect different kinds of creatures makes containing their spread particularly difficult. Even when an established population is eliminated, a single infected individual can potentially establish a new outbreak elsewhere.

Abilities

  • Curse Strike: Their attacks can transmit the curse to their victims, potentially beginning the transformation into a new Curse Beast.
  • Powerful Musculature: Most manifestations possess considerable physical strength, making them dangerous even without relying on their magical abilities.
  • Adaptability: The curse can develop different manifestations depending on the creatures it infects, allowing Curse Beasts to survive in a wide variety of environments.
  • Immunity to Lesser Curses: Their bodies are highly resistant to minor curses and similar magical afflictions, making many conventional curse-based attacks ineffective.

Ecology

Curse Beasts are generally considered a plague wherever they appear. Unlike many other artificial creatures, they have little place in the natural world and often cause severe ecological disturbances.

Their aggression and compulsion to spread the curse result in unnecessary killings, with many victims left behind. As the infection spreads through local animal populations, it can disrupt existing ecosystems and threaten entire communities of wildlife.

Some magical creatures have developed ways to deal with Curse Beasts. Depending on the species, these adaptations can include ambushing them, concealing their magical signatures, or simply possessing enough aggression and physical strength to defend themselves.

Relationship with Humans

Curse Beasts were originally created by humans as weapons of war, but their inability to distinguish between allies and enemies made them a danger to everyone around them. Their creators eventually lost control of the creatures, allowing the curse to spread beyond the battlefield.

Their hostility towards humanity is a direct consequence of their original design. The curse was created to target humans, and this directive remains even after the creatures have spread far beyond their original purpose.

For centuries, the various nations of Drakma have attempted to exterminate them, seeking to contain their populations and prevent further outbreaks. The Hunters Guild has played a major role in these efforts, organizing hunts and monitoring reports of new infestations.

Despite these efforts, completely eradicating Curse Beasts has proven extremely difficult. Their ability to infect new hosts means that even successful extermination campaigns can only slow their spread. Nevertheless, these efforts have made attacks on human settlements less frequent and reduced their numbers in many regions.

Trivia

  • The original Curse Beasts were created during the War of the Three Crowns.
  • Werewolves are considered the original manifestation of the curse, with other variants appearing as it spread to different animals.
  • Humans are particularly vulnerable to infection, but also tend to remain infected longer than other hosts.
  • A fully transformed Curse Beast retains no meaningful memories or consciousness from its former life.
