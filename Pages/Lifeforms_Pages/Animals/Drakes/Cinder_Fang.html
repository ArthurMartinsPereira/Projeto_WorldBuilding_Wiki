Overview

The mighty Jungle Tyrants of Falkore are among the largest species of drake in Drakma and the tallest currently known. These enormous herbivores inhabit the nation's dense jungles, where they travel in family herds led by the oldest and most experienced members. Despite their peaceful nature, they can become extremely dangerous when threatened, particularly when protecting their young. A small number of Falkorean clans have developed secretive techniques for domesticating them.

Appearance

Jungle Tyrants are enormous, sauropod-like drakes with long necks and tails. Their tails can reach almost twice the length of their bodies, giving them an enormous reach when defending themselves. Like other drakes, they naturally grow mineralized scale armor, although Jungle Tyrants possess considerably less of it than many other drake species due to the scarcity of minerals in their natural habitats. Their armor primarily covers the shoulders, spine, legs and base of the neck.

The final section of their tail is covered in overlapping metallic scales that form a flexible cutting edge. These scales are extremely sharp and allow the Jungle Tyrant to use its tail as a powerful whip, capable of inflicting devastating injuries. The scales naturally wear down through use, so Jungle Tyrants frequently sharpen them against exposed rocks or the mineralized portions of their own armor.

Their scales usually range from various shades of green to brown, helping them blend into the vegetation of the jungle. Their mineralized armor and tail blades are generally darker and have a metallic appearance.

Males are generally larger and more brightly colored than females. They possess specialized fins and air sacs along their necks, which become particularly prominent during mating season. These structures are used for visual displays intended to attract mates and intimidate rival males.

Behavior

Jungle Tyrants are generally calm and social animals that travel in family herds ranging from a few individuals to much larger groups. Herds are usually led by their oldest and most experienced members, regardless of sex. These individuals guide migrations, select nesting grounds and help protect younger members of the herd.

They prefer to establish their nests in elevated areas close to reliable sources of fresh water. High ground protects the nests from flooding and gives the adults a better view of the surrounding jungle. Their nests are communal structures made from large rocks, branches, logs, mud and other materials. Older nests can become enormous structures resembling hollowed-out hills and may be reused by the same herd for generations.

The entire herd participates in maintaining the nest and caring for the young. Adults will often remain close to the nesting area while younger members are present, becoming particularly aggressive toward predators or unfamiliar creatures that approach too closely. This behavior has caused most large predators to avoid Jungle Tyrant herds whenever possible.

Jungle Tyrants usually spread out while feeding, allowing individuals to browse through different layers of vegetation without competing directly with one another. Adults often break branches or knock down fruit for younger members of the herd, and the largest individuals may clear dense vegetation while feeding, creating temporary feeding grounds.

During mating season, males compete primarily through displays of size, coloration and the development of their neck fins. Females generally favor males with brighter colors and larger, more impressive fins. Serious fights are uncommon, as injuries between mature males could weaken the herd, but rival males may attempt to damage each other's fins in order to reduce their chances of attracting mates.

Both males and females carefully maintain their tail blades, as these are their primary weapons. A severely damaged tail or neck fin can affect an individual's position within the herd.

Solitary Jungle Tyrants are uncommon. Most are young individuals that have recently left their original herd or older individuals that have been expelled. Particularly aggressive males may also become pariahs after repeatedly attacking members of their own herd. Prolonged isolation can have a severe effect on these highly social animals, often making pariahs increasingly aggressive and unpredictable.

Habitat

Jungle Tyrants are native to the jungles and forests of Falkore and are not naturally found anywhere else. They favor regions with dense vegetation, especially areas containing abundant fruit-bearing trees and reliable sources of fresh water. They have also been observed consuming honey from the massive beehives found throughout the jungles of Falkore. Their thick skin and mineralized scales provide some protection against the bees, allowing them to feed from hives that would be inaccessible to most other animals.

Their enormous size and feeding habits have a noticeable effect on their surroundings. Herds create wide paths as they move through dense vegetation, while their feeding opens gaps in the jungle canopy and allows smaller plants to grow. These paths are often reused by other animals and, in some regions, have become important routes for human travel.

Jungle Tyrants also periodically travel to mineral-rich locations, where they consume mineral-rich soil and small stones. These locations provide materials necessary for the growth and maintenance of their mineralized armor and tail blades. Some of these mineral deposits are visited by several herds and have become important landmarks within their migration routes. For rocks that are too large to swallow, they instead lick their surfaces with their rough tongues, absorbing some of the minerals from them. They do the same if they find exposed deposits on rock walls or cliffsides. Over time these rocks and walls might even look somewhat polished.

Life Cycle

Jungle Tyrants reproduce seasonally, with mating season coinciding with periods when fruit and foliage are particularly abundant. Herds may travel considerable distances to reach regions with sufficient food and suitable nesting grounds.

Young Jungle Tyrants, known as drakelings, remain within communal nests and are cared for by the entire herd rather than only by their parents. Older drakes help protect them while their parents forage. Drakelings are considerably smaller than adults and possess only limited mineralized armor. Their tail blades also develop gradually as they mature. These younger drakes often consume roots, fruits or foliage brought to them by their parents. After hatching, drakelings consume the remains of their mineralized eggshells, allowing them to recover valuable minerals that are used in the development of their first armor.

Jungle Tyrants grow throughout much of their lives, with the largest individuals being exceptionally old. The development of their mineralized armor and tail blades continues as they mature, while males gradually develop their characteristic neck fins and air sacs. Jungle Tyrants can live for up to 150 years, although such lifespans are rare and most individuals die before reaching 100.

Young drakes remain dependent on their parents and other adults for around six years, after which they become increasingly independent. Despite this, they usually remain with their birth herd until reaching approximately 16 years of age. At this point, some leave to establish their own herds, while others join existing groups. This dispersal prevents excessive competition for resources and reduces inbreeding, with individuals either joining another herd or forming new groups with other dispersing adults.

Abilities

  • Tail Whip: Their enormous tails can be swung with tremendous force. The metallic scales at the tip form a flexible cutting edge capable of causing devastating injuries.
  • Geo Armor: Jungle Tyrants can channel Geo energy through their mineralized scales, temporarily reinforcing their natural armor and making them significantly more resistant to physical attacks.
  • Animus Enhancement: Animus energy can be circulated through their bodies to enhance their already immense physical strength and endurance.
  • Elemental Shockwave: By stomping the ground and releasing elemental energy through their limbs, Jungle Tyrants can create powerful shockwaves capable of knocking nearby creatures off their feet.
  • Titanic Strength: Jungle Tyrants possess extraordinary physical strength and are considered one of the strongest naturally occurring species in Drakma.

Ecology

Jungle Tyrants are among the best-known animals of Falkore and are considered an important part of the nation's natural heritage. Their immense size and feeding habits allow them to influence large portions of the jungle ecosystem. By consuming vegetation and breaking through dense growth, they create clearings and pathways that are later used by smaller animals.

Many of the clearings and paths that cut through the jungles of Falkore were originally created by generations of Jungle Tyrant herds moving between feeding and nesting grounds. For centuries, humans have used some of these paths as rudimentary roads through otherwise difficult terrain.

Their communal nests can also become permanent features of the landscape. The oldest nests may be maintained by generations of the same herd, gradually growing larger as new layers of rocks, branches, logs and mud are added.

Because of their ecological importance and their cultural significance, Jungle Tyrants are protected by many Falkorean communities. Hunting them is generally considered dangerous and, in some regions, taboo.

Relationship with Humans

The relationship between humans and Jungle Tyrants is one of both respect and fear. For generations, the animals have been regarded as symbols of power and strength, often being described as the undisputed kings of the jungle. Although attacks on settlements and caravans are rare, their enormous strength makes even an accidental confrontation dangerous.

A small number of Falkorean clans have spent generations developing techniques for communicating with and taming Jungle Tyrants. These techniques are closely guarded and are generally passed from one generation to the next. Rather than completely domesticating the species, these clans form long-term bonds with individual animals and occasionally entire herds.

Semi-domesticated Jungle Tyrants can be trained to transport people and heavy cargo through the jungle, making them valuable alternatives to conventional pack animals in difficult terrain. Experienced handlers can also use their knowledge of herd behavior to safely guide them along established routes.

The military of Falkore has also adopted the use of these animals, primarily for logistics and transportation. In some circumstances, trained Jungle Tyrants may also be used in combat, where their immense strength and elemental abilities make them formidable battlefield assets.

Trivia

  • Jungle Tyrants are featured on the banner of the Forest Watch and are considered one of the symbols of Falkore.
  • Some of the oldest Jungle Tyrant nests are large enough to be mistaken for natural hills from a distance.
  • Jungle Tyrant migration routes have been used as roads by Falkorean communities for centuries.
  • Their tail blades are continuously maintained and sharpened throughout their lives.
  • Experienced Falkorean handlers can recognize individual Jungle Tyrants by their coloration, scars, fins and tail blades.
